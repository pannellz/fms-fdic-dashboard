## Purpose

This document provides community banking industry context for AI-generated analyst memos.

## Year-by-Year Summary (2020-2025)

### 2020

The COVID-19 pandemic triggered a 3.5% GDP contraction, with unemployment spiking to 13.0% in Q2 before easing to 6.7% by year-end. The Fed slashed the federal funds rate to 0-0.25% in March. Despite the recession, community banks reported aggregate net income of $25.9 billion (up 3.6% from 2019), buoyed by PPP fee income and noninterest income that jumped 33.7% YoY. Community bank ROA averaged roughly 1.20% for the full year (pretax ROA ranged from 1.02% in Q1 to 1.42% in Q4). NIM compressed from approximately 3.55% in Q1 to 3.32% in Q4 as near-zero rates took hold. Deposits surged 18.4% YoY as stimulus checks and PPP flooded the system, pushing loan-to-deposit ratios to historic lows (approximately 55-60%). Loan growth was 10.3% YoY, almost entirely driven by PPP originations (C&I loans up 52.8%). Noncurrent loans held at 0.77%, roughly flat with 2019, and industry net charge-offs fell approximately 20% YoY, though many expected losses were deferred by forbearance programs. Loan-loss provisions rose 142% YoY as banks built reserves. The number of FDIC-insured community banks fell from approximately 4,623 to 4,559. The CARES Act and PPP were the dominant industry events; community banks originated roughly one-third of all PPP loans nationally.

### 2021

GDP rebounded strongly (approximately 5.7%), unemployment fell from 8% to 4%, and inflation began rising above 5% by year-end. The Fed held rates at 0-0.25% throughout the year. Community banks earned $32.7 billion in net income (up 29.3% YoY). Industry ROA reached approximately 1.23%, the highest in a decade. Community bank NIM was approximately 3.20%, down about 11 bps from 2020, reflecting the full-year impact of near-zero rates. Deposits grew approximately 10-13% (community banks specifically up 13.5% in the year ending June 2021). Loan growth was only 2.0% YoY at community banks due to PPP forgiveness and payoffs; excluding PPP, underlying loan growth was approximately 7.6%. Asset quality was exceptional: noncurrent loans fell to a record low of 0.58% and net charge-offs were negligible at 0.06%. Provision expense turned negative as banks released reserves. CRE lending became the primary growth engine, expanding over 10% YoY, while C&I was flat after PPP runoff. M&A was muted through early 2021 but picked up in H2 as economic clarity returned. Branch closures accelerated, with community banks closing approximately 14% of metro branches over 2016-2021.

### 2022

Real GDP growth slowed to 2.1%, unemployment stayed near historic lows at approximately 3.5%, and inflation peaked at 9.1% in June before easing to 6.5% by year-end. The Fed raised the federal funds rate aggressively from near-zero to 4.25-4.50% by December, the fastest tightening in decades. Community bank ROA was approximately 1.12% (industry ROA approximately 1.12%), with earnings roughly flat as rising funding costs offset loan growth. NIM widened to approximately 3.71% by Q4 as loan yields repriced higher faster than deposit costs. Loan growth was robust at approximately 14.4% YoY at community banks, driven by C&I, consumer, and CRE lending. Deposit growth decelerated sharply to approximately 4.8% for the year ending mid-2022, and deposits actually declined 0.7% in Q4 as excess pandemic savings were drawn down. Asset quality remained strong: noncurrent loans at a record low of 0.44% and net charge-offs at 0.11%. No banks failed in 2022, extending a 28-month streak. M&A cooled from 2021's pace, with 149 deals announced through November versus 193 the prior year. The Midwest led in deal activity. The FTX crypto collapse triggered massive deposit withdrawals at specialty banks (Silvergate lost 68% of deposits in Q4), foreshadowing liquidity vulnerabilities that would emerge in 2023.

### 2023

GDP growth was moderate, unemployment remained low, and inflation continued easing. The Fed held rates at the 5.25-5.50% peak through most of the year. The spring banking crisis dominated: Silicon Valley Bank, Signature Bank, and First Republic Bank failed in March-May, driven by concentrated uninsured deposits, duration mismatches, and confidence runs. The Fed created the Bank Term Funding Program, and Treasury/Fed/FDIC invoked a systemic risk exception to protect all depositors at SVB and Signature. The FDIC later finalized a special assessment to recoup losses, exempting banks under $5 billion in assets. Community bank full-year ROA fell to 1.01% (from 1.15% in 2022), ROE to 10.74%, and NIM compressed to 3.39%. Net income was $26.6 billion. Loan growth was 2.1% on reported balances (7.8% merger-adjusted). Deposits declined approximately 2.0% on reported balances as funding competition intensified. Asset quality weakened modestly: noncurrent assets plus OREO rose to 0.40% and net charge-offs to 0.11%. Capital remained strong with leverage ratios at 10.70%. Regionally, Kansas City community banks had only 2.31% unprofitable institutions versus 10.20% in San Francisco and 9.40% in New York. M&A was subdued: 91 announced deals through November versus 144 in the prior year, with 93 community banks merging out of existence across the four quarters. Heartland Tri-State Bank in Kansas ($139 million) was the only true community bank failure, caused by a cryptocurrency fraud.

### 2024

Real GDP grew 2.8%, CPI was up 2.9% YoY by December, and labor markets remained firm. The Fed held rates restrictive through August, then cut 100 bps total across September, November, and December meetings, ending the year at 4.25-4.50%. Community bank ROA fell to 0.95%, ROE to 9.59%, and NIM compressed to 3.33%. Net income declined 3.0% to $25.9 billion. Interest expense rose 39.0% and provision expense rose 21.1%. Loan growth was 3.2% (strongest in home equity lines at +12.6% and farm loans at +6.5%; C&I grew only 0.8%). Deposits grew 2.9%, with brokered deposits up 9.7% but other borrowed funds down 18.1% as emergency liquidity from 2023 unwound. Credit quality deteriorated: noncurrent loans rose 30.0% YoY, noncurrent assets plus OREO reached 0.52%, and net charge-offs rose to 0.16%. Regional dispersion was stark: Dallas community banks posted 1.19% ROA and 3.70% NIM versus New York at 0.59% ROA and 2.88% NIM. The share of unprofitable institutions reached 13.88% in San Francisco and 12.90% in New York versus 2.91% in Kansas City. Republic First Bank ($6 billion) failed in April, driven by HTM securities reclassification and unrealized losses. Community bank count fell from 4,128 to 4,046. M&A rose modestly to 125 announced deals from 98 in 2023. Credit-union acquisitions of banks hit a record 17 deals.

### 2025

Real GDP grew 2.1%, CPI was up 2.7% YoY by December, and payroll growth slowed to 49,000 per month. The Fed lowered the funds rate to 3.50-3.75% by year-end. Community banks staged an earnings recovery: net income rose to $29.9 billion (up 22.5% merger-adjusted), ROA improved to 1.11%, ROE to 10.65%, and NIM widened to 3.65% (Q4 NIM reached 3.77%). Funding costs fell faster than asset yields late in the year. Merger-adjusted loan growth was 5.4% and deposit growth was 5.0%. Brokered deposits fell 3.7% and FHLB advances fell 9.8% as funding normalized. However, credit costs continued rising: noncurrent loans rose 19.3% (merger-adjusted), net charge-offs rose 30.0%, and noncurrent assets plus OREO reached 0.61% with net charge-offs at 0.21% of loans. Community bank count fell to 3,909. Completed community bank mergers rose to 127, the highest since 2021; sellers had median ROAA of 0.6% versus 1.1% for buyers. Two community banks failed (Pulaski Savings Bank in Chicago, governance/control failure; Santa Anna National Bank in Texas, suspected fraud). Regulators proposed rescinding the 2023 CRA modernization rule, easing the CBLR framework to 8% from 9%, and issuing tailored BSA/AML exam procedures for community banks. FedNow reached 1,600 participating institutions. Core-provider dissatisfaction was high, with only 53% of banks satisfied.

## Key Themes an Analyst Should Know

### The Deposit Surge of 2020-2021

Community bank deposits surged 18.4% in 2020 and another 10-13% in 2021, driven by three forces: (1) direct stimulus payments to consumers via the CARES Act and American Rescue Plan, (2) PPP loan proceeds deposited into business accounts, and (3) reduced consumer spending during lockdowns. Noninterest-bearing deposits grew fastest (up approximately 26% in 2020). This created record liquidity and loan-to-deposit ratios near 55-60%, but much of this deposit base was temporary or rate-insensitive only as long as rates stayed near zero. When rates rose in 2022-2023, the deposit base began repricing or leaving, creating the funding pressure that defined 2023-2024.

### Margin Compression and Recovery (2020-2025)

NIM followed a distinct arc. Margins compressed in 2020-2021 (from approximately 3.5% to 3.2%) as the Fed held rates at zero and banks accumulated excess liquidity they could not profitably deploy. In 2022, margins widened sharply as loan yields repriced higher faster than deposit costs, reaching approximately 3.71% by Q4 2022. Then margins compressed again through 2023-2024 (to 3.33% by 2024) as deposit betas caught up, competition for deposits intensified after the spring 2023 bank failures, and banks turned to more expensive wholesale funding. Recovery began in 2025 as the Fed eased and funding costs fell faster than asset yields, pushing NIM back to 3.65%. The key insight: community banks experienced a double compression (first from zero rates, then from lagged deposit repricing) before margins finally stabilized.

### The CRE Concentration Risk Story

Community banks are structurally concentrated in commercial real estate. At year-end 2023, real-estate-secured loans were 78.3% of community bank total loans, with nonfarm nonresidential CRE at 30.8%. At year-end 2024, 30% of community banks were CRE-concentrated by regulatory definitions, and community banks held 29% of all CRE loans despite holding only 15% of total industry loans. The risk played out unevenly: community banks generally had low exposure to large urban office properties (the most stressed CRE segment), which limited their direct credit losses. However, regulators consistently flagged CRE as the top concentration risk throughout 2022-2025. The practical risk for individual community banks depends heavily on local market conditions, property type mix, and the refinancing challenge for loans originated at lower rates that must now be serviced at higher coupons.

### M&A and Consolidation

The community banking sector continued a decades-long consolidation trend, falling from approximately 4,559 institutions in 2020 to 3,909 in 2025 (a 14% reduction over five years). M&A was muted in 2020-early 2021 due to pandemic uncertainty, accelerated in late 2021, pulled back in 2022-2023 due to rate volatility and valuation uncertainty, and rebounded modestly in 2024-2025. By 2025, completed community bank mergers reached 127 (highest since 2021), and the data showed clear economic sorting: sellers had median ROAA of 0.6% and loan growth of 0.5%, versus 1.1% ROAA and 4.7% loan growth for buyers. Credit-union acquisitions of banks became a notable trend, hitting a record 17 deals in 2024. Consolidation is driven by scale economics (technology costs, compliance burden, succession), not distress, but the pace is accelerating as fixed costs rise.

### Regional Performance Differences

Geography consistently drove meaningful performance variation. Throughout 2020-2025, community banks in the Dallas/South and Kansas City/Midwest regions generally outperformed on profitability and stability. Kansas City consistently posted the lowest share of unprofitable institutions (2.31% in 2023, 2.91% in 2024), reflecting stable agricultural and energy economies and conservative balance sheets. Dallas posted the highest ROA in 2024 (1.19%) with strong NIMs. New York and San Francisco consistently lagged, with the highest unprofitable shares (10-14% range in 2023-2024) due to thinner margins, office CRE exposure, and higher-beta deposit bases. Within regions, agricultural banks faced divergent conditions: crop-heavy areas (Midwest/Plains) showed stress in 2025, while cattle-producing areas remained healthier. The key takeaway for individual bank analysis: national averages mask significant local variation, and a bank's specific market economics matter more than sector-wide trends.

### The 2023 Banking Crisis and Its Aftermath

The failures of Silicon Valley Bank, Signature Bank, and First Republic Bank in spring 2023 were not community bank failures, but they reshaped the entire sector. The crisis forced every community bank to reassess uninsured deposit concentrations, liquidity contingency plans, interest-rate risk management, and HTM securities portfolios. The Fed's Bank Term Funding Program (where approximately 95% of borrowers had less than $10 billion in assets) provided critical liquidity. The FDIC's special assessment exempted banks under $5 billion in uninsured deposits, shielding most community banks from direct cost. The lasting effect was a permanent increase in supervisory expectations around liquidity planning, deposit concentration analysis, and rate-risk governance.
