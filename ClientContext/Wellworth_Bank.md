# ClientContext: Wellworth Bank (Citizens Bank)
### Brand Discovery Report Summary — FMS, May 2024

---

> **Note on Naming:** The report was prepared for **Citizens Bank** under its then-current branding. The client is now operating as **Wellworth Bank** following the rebrand. All references to "Citizens Bank" or its subsidiary names below reflect the pre-rebrand state described in the research.

---

## Background & History

Citizens Bank is a Middle Tennessee community bank with roots exceeding 100 years of continuous operation. The parent entity, Citizens Bank (headquartered in **Lafayette, Macon County, TN**), traces its formal history to **1909**, though the collective of affiliated banks includes institutions operating for **over 120 years**. The bank crossed the **$1 billion asset threshold** in recent years and stood at approximately **$1.6 billion in assets** at the time of the report (May 2024). Since 2018 alone, asset size increased **over 68%**, driven primarily by acquisitions.

The bank has grown through a series of acquisitions and mergers, resulting in the operation of **six separate bank brands** under one holding company:

1. **Citizens Bank** (Lafayette / Macon County — headquarters)
2. **American Bank and Trust (AB&T)** — Cookeville market
3. **Bank of Celina**
4. **Liberty State Bank**
5. **Smith County Bank**
6. **Traders Bank** — Tullahoma market

Each entity retained its own name, logo, and operational identity post-acquisition, producing significant brand fragmentation. One executive summarized the state of the organization plainly: *"In 10 years, I hope we act like a $2.5 billion bank. Today we act like six $250 million banks."*

---

## Geographic Footprint & Market Characteristics

- **22 branch locations** spanning a **100+ mile corridor of eastern Middle Tennessee**
- **Primary markets served:** Lafayette, Lebanon, Cookeville, Tullahoma, Celina, and surrounding smaller/rural communities in Smith County and Macon County
- Markets are characterized as **smaller towns and more rural communities**, with the bank occupying a dominant local presence in Macon County but significantly lower awareness and market penetration in Lebanon, Cookeville, and Tullahoma
- The branch network is a **largely untapped strategic asset** — multiple customers reported driving one or two towns past a Citizens-affiliated branch, unaware they could transact there
- Citizens Bank is **strongest in Macon County (Lafayette)**; marketing investment and brand recognition drop substantially in outlying markets
- **Branch traffic remains relatively high** compared to industry norms — nearly half of surveyed customers still visit branches regularly, some as often as weekly

---

## Organizational Structure & Leadership

- The organization is structured as a **multi-brand holding company** with centralized back-office operations (Lafayette) and decentralized front-line banking under six different brand names
- **Executive team** is based primarily in Lafayette; centralization of operations has created tension with branch-level staff across acquired markets
- Approximately **275 total employees** referenced across the organization (one executive cited "the finest 275 people around")
- **One person is reportedly responsible for training** across the entire organization — a significant capacity constraint given the scale of the footprint
- Departments referenced include: Retail Banking, Business Banking, Mortgage, Marketing, HR, IT, and a centralized Customer Service/Escrow function
- **Board of Directors** participated in the brand discovery survey process
- The **IT department** was noted as maintaining strong customer data and reporting capabilities, though Marketing had not been actively utilizing these reports for targeting or cross-sell campaigns

## Strategic Context

### Why the Rebrand
The rebrand (to Wellworth Bank) is driven by the need to consolidate six separate brand identities into a **single, unified brand**. The fragmentation of operating under six names was identified as the most frequently discussed internal challenge — creating customer confusion, operational inconsistency, and an inability to leverage the full branch network as a competitive asset. Employees across every market expressed readiness and desire to operate as one unified institution.

Key triggering factors for the brand consolidation:
- Customers receiving statements from unfamiliar bank names, not recognizing them as their own bank
- Calls routed to branches in other towns under different names, creating confusion and friction
- Loss of commercial relationships to competitors because prospects only saw "two little branches" rather than the full 22-branch network
- Inability to project the bank's true size ($1.6B asset) and capability in the marketplace

### Growth Strategy
- Executives expressed a goal of reaching **$2.5 billion in assets** within approximately 10 years
- Growth levers include: **deposit acquisition** (particularly low-cost deposits to fund loan growth), **commercial/business banking expansion**, and **mortgage lending**
- The bank has been predominantly reactive to market rate movements — a specific example cited was losing deposit volume to **Wilson Bank** and **Pinnacle** when rates rose, because Citizens waited too long to act
- Unifying under one brand is viewed as foundational to enabling a more proactive, coordinated growth strategy

### Target Segments
- **Primary retail banking customers** in smaller Middle Tennessee markets (existing base skews toward established, relationship-oriented customers)
- **Small-to-medium business owners** (commercial/business banking is a key growth priority)
- **Younger demographics (Gen Z and Millennials)** identified as a critical but underserved segment — current technology gaps (no Zelle, limited tap-to-pay, no online account opening) are specific barriers to attracting this group
- **Mortgage borrowers** across the regional footprint

## Competitive Positioning & Differentiation

### Core Differentiators (Validated by Customers and Employees)
- **Hometown feel at scale:** Described repeatedly as feeling like a small local bank despite being a $1.6B institution; customers know staff by name and vice versa
- **Local decision-making:** Ability to make same-day loan decisions and find flexible, customized solutions — cited by customers as a distinct advantage over larger banks
- **Relational banking:** Customers referenced the ability to "pick up the phone and call someone directly" as a primary reason for loyalty
- **Community involvement:** Visible in school sponsorships, local causes, and civic engagement — recognized even by noncustomers
- **Branch network breadth:** 22 locations across a 100-mile corridor is a significant but undermarketed convenience advantage

### Named Competitors
- **Wilson Bank & Trust** — referenced most frequently as the primary community bank benchmark; described as more proactive, better marketed, more community-visible, and offering products like Zelle; employees actively lost deposits to Wilson Bank when rate actions were delayed
- **Pinnacle Financial Partners** — named in the context of deposit losses during rising rate environment
- **Regions Bank** — referenced by an employee who previously worked there; used as a negative contrast ("how can't we" vs. Citizens' "how can we" attitude)
- **Smart Bank** — cited in context of a staff member who left Citizens for $6/hour more
- **Other unnamed community banks** referenced as doing more marketing and community activation in Lebanon, Cookeville, and Tullahoma markets

### Positioning Gap
The bank is perceived by noncustomers as **too small to meet their needs** — despite a 22-branch network and $1.6B in assets. This perception gap is the most actionable competitive vulnerability. The bank is not being considered for larger commercial relationships or full banking relationships because of incorrect size assumptions.

## Market Dynamics

### Economic Conditions & Rate Environment
- The rising interest rate environment (referenced in context of 2022–2023 Fed rate cycle) exposed the bank's **reactive deposit pricing strategy**, resulting in lost deposit market share to Wilson Bank and Pinnacle
- Employee savings/personal deposits were cited as having gone to competitors before Citizens offered competitive rates — a credibility-damaging data point internally

### Threats
- **Talent attrition to competitors:** Staff being recruited away by Smart Bank, Wilson Bank, and others at materially higher wages; one teller cited losing a colleague for $6/hour more
- **Technology expectations gap:** Gen Z and Millennials benchmarking all digital experiences against Amazon/Apple; Citizens lags on Zelle, tap-to-pay debit, and online account opening — potentially disqualifying the bank as a primary institution for younger customers
- **Competitor marketing intensity:** Community bank competitors (some smaller than Citizens) are described as more present on social media, more active in communities outside Macon County, and more visible on billboards and radio in key growth markets
- **Fee structure:** The absence of a free checking product is specifically cited as a barrier to new customer acquisition; multiple voices noted that charging $4/month for checking disqualifies Citizens from consideration when national banks offer free accounts with direct deposit

### Opportunities
- **Brand unification:** Consolidating to a single name/brand is projected to immediately unlock the value of 22 branches as a regional network, reduce customer confusion, and enable coordinated marketing
- **Deposit growth:** Low-cost checking deposits are the stated priority; a free or low-barrier consumer checking product is seen as essential
- **Cross-sell marketing:** IT has robust customer data available; Marketing has not yet leveraged it — an immediate, low-cost opportunity to deepen existing relationships
- **Noncustomer conversion:** Noncustomers who know the bank primarily describe it positively (community supporter, good people) but report no product awareness or engagement — a significant acquisition opportunity with relatively low brand resistance
- **Commercial/business banking:** Local decision-making capability is a genuine differentiator for business clients; multiple stories of winning commercial relationships that larger banks couldn't service as quickly

## Brand Perception

### Customer Views
- Customers are **highly loyal and satisfied** with personal relationships and service quality
- Key loyalty drivers: knowing staff by name, responsiveness, personal phone access to bankers, and the ability to solve unusual problems quickly (e.g., same-day cashier's check for out-of-state equipment purchase)
- Pain points noted: **turnover** (customers notice new faces and express concern), **inconsistent experience across branches** (one customer noted a very different feeling visiting Smith County Bank vs. Liberty State Bank), and **hours** (8–5 weekday hours with inconsistent Saturday access make it difficult for working customers)
- Average surveyed customer uses **3.0 different banks** — Citizens is often not the sole banking relationship, creating vulnerability and opportunity
- Technology is generally viewed positively for basic functions (mobile app, online banking); gaps in Zelle and tap-to-pay are noted but not deal-breakers for existing customers

### Employee Views
- Employees are **passionate about service and community** but frustrated by operational constraints
- ~80% of surveyed employees report strong team spirit and camaraderie
- Top internal frustrations: lack of empowerment to make decisions, poor inter-bank communication, inconsistent processes, below-market compensation, and high turnover creating workload strain
- Employees in non-Lafayette markets (Cookeville, Lebanon, Tullahoma) feel **invisible to leadership** — marketing support, recognition, and communication are perceived as heavily skewed toward Macon County
- The recognition program (highlighting account-opening volumes in quarterly meetings) is seen as structurally unfair to smaller branches and non-sales roles; it is actively contributing to morale issues
- Employees express **willingness to unify and execute** once leadership provides a clear direction: *"We need to find a way to be proficient, efficient, productive, profitable and we run with it"*

### Noncustomer Views
- Brand awareness outside Macon County is **very low**; noncustomers in Lebanon, Cookeville, and Tullahoma report hearing and seeing almost nothing from the bank
- Primary perception among noncustomers: the bank is **small, limited in technology, and likely fee-heavy** — all inaccurate assumptions
- Even noncustomers who view the bank favorably (community-oriented, locally trusted) cite lack of information as the reason they haven't considered switching
- Noncustomers use an average of **3.5 different banks**, suggesting high openness to additional banking relationships if the value proposition is clearly communicated
- Name confusion is a documented noncustomer barrier: multiple "Citizens Bank"-branded competitors in the same markets create identity confusion before any engagement occurs

## Key Takeaways for Financial Analysis

### Deposit Drivers & Risks
- **Lost deposit opportunity** is a documented and quantified problem: the bank missed rate-cycle deposit inflows that went to Wilson Bank and Pinnacle due to reactive pricing
- **Free checking** is identified as the single most frequently cited product gap — multiple executives and employees believe the $4/month fee is directly costing new account acquisition
- **Cross-sell gap:** IT has customer data; Marketing isn't using it — deposit deepening with existing customers is an immediate, low-investment opportunity
- **Deposit funding pressure** is implicit in the executive goal to "look bigger to get more deposits" — the bank needs brand credibility upgrades to compete for larger commercial deposit relationships

### Branch Plans
- No new branches announced in the report, but the **existing 22-branch network is significantly underutilized** from a marketing and customer awareness standpoint
- Branch facility condition is a documented concern — executive-level comments about the Lebanon office exterior/interior; employee reports of maintenance issues (roaches, unpaved parking) at specific locations; branch refresh/renovation investment is recommended as part of brand rollout
- **Inconsistent branch hours** across the six bank entities are an operational and customer experience problem requiring standardization post-rebrand
- Saturday support infrastructure is described as critically inadequate — back office, IT, and approval authorities are not available on Saturdays despite some branches being open

### Financial Goals
- **Target asset size:** ~$2.5 billion (from ~$1.6 billion), implying a **~56% growth target** on an unspecified timeline (implied ~10 years by executive comment)
- **Primary growth engines identified:** deposit acquisition (low-cost consumer and commercial checking), commercial loan growth, and mortgage origination
- **Compensation competitiveness** is a financial priority — below-market pay is producing turnover that creates measurable efficiency losses and customer relationship disruption; investment in compensation is framed as a brand and retention investment
- **Technology investment gaps** — Zelle, tap-to-pay, online account opening, and internal system integration are all cited as revenue-impacting deficiencies; internal systems requiring four-to-five touch entries per transaction represent a quantifiable inefficiency cost
- The bank's **conservative, relationship-driven underwriting culture** is an asset — customer stories of flexible, fast lending decisions confirm the bank is winning deals that larger banks cannot service; this should be preserved and marketed aggressively as brand and growth unification proceeds
