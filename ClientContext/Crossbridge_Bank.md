# ClientContext: Tomahawk Community Bank
## Brand Discovery Report Summary — FMS, August 2021

---

## Background & History

**Tomahawk Community Bank** is a **mutual savings institution** headquartered in Tomahawk, Wisconsin, with roots dating to **1920** — giving it over 100 years of continuous operation in Northern Wisconsin. The bank has weathered world wars, economic crises, and the COVID-19 pandemic through what leadership describes as responsible, prudent decision-making.

Approximately **five years prior to this report** (circa 2016), Tomahawk Community Bank merged with **Merrill Federal Savings and Loan** (operating as **Merrill Community Bank**). At the time of merger, leadership deliberately preserved both names and allowed the two banks to operate with significant independence, honoring the distinct community identities of Tomahawk and Merrill, Wisconsin. As of August 2021, the post-merger integration remains incomplete from a cultural, leadership, and brand standpoint — despite five years having elapsed. The organization is currently pursuing a **full rebrand**, including a new unified name (**Crossbridge Bank**, per the FMS engagement context), new visual identity, and consolidated brand strategy.

Merrill Community Bank was originally founded with the specific mission of facilitating home ownership for area residents. Tomahawk Community Bank historically developed stronger ties to tourism-driven mortgage lending in the northern Wisconsin lake country.

---

## Geographic Footprint & Market Characteristics

- **Primary markets:** Tomahawk, Wisconsin and Merrill, Wisconsin (Lincoln County area, Northern Wisconsin)
- Both markets are described as **small, close-knit communities** where personal relationships, civic identity, and local reputation carry significant weight in banking decisions
- The Tomahawk market benefits from **tourism activity** (lakes region), which drives meaningful mortgage lending volume — particularly with builders and realtors
- Merrill has historically been more **commercially oriented**, with Merrill Federal Savings and Loan rooted in residential lending but the combined entity leaning into commercial banking
- Leadership acknowledged that the bank's **biggest growth opportunities lie outside its legacy markets** — a key driver of the rebrand and brand unification effort
- The bank currently operates at minimum **two branch locations** (Tomahawk and Merrill); no additional branch count is specified in the report

---

## Organizational Structure & Leadership

- **Mutual charter:** The bank is owned by its depositors, not shareholders. This structure is cited repeatedly as a strategic and cultural differentiator — enabling long-term decision-making, capital retention, and independence from dividend pressure.
- **Board of Directors:** Described by FMS as unusually active and engaged relative to peer community banks nationwide. Board members bring diverse backgrounds, make direct customer referrals, and participate actively in bank committees. Characterized as well-connected and respected in their communities.
- **Dual leadership structure (critical challenge):** The merger produced two co-existing leadership styles and management philosophies — one rooted in Tomahawk, one in Merrill. As of the report date, these have **not been reconciled**, producing an internal "us vs. them" cultural dynamic. Employees and even one noncustomer focus group participant noted the visible lack of unified direction.
- **Staffing profile:** A significant portion of staff has tenures exceeding **20 years**. The bank faces an **upcoming wave of retirements**, creating knowledge-transfer risk and succession planning urgency.
- **Monthly community meetings** are in place as an internal communication tool but were noted as needing structural improvement to be more informative and mission-aligned.

## Strategic Context

### Why the Rebrand?
The rebrand is driven by several converging pressures:

1. **Post-merger brand fragmentation:** Five years after the Tomahawk/Merrill merger, the bank functionally still operates as two brands in the minds of customers, noncustomers, and to a meaningful degree, employees. Confusion persists about the bank's name, the relationship between the two entities, and the organization's direction.
2. **Growth ambition beyond legacy markets:** Leadership explicitly recognizes that organic growth in Tomahawk and Merrill is limited. A unified, modernized brand is seen as prerequisite to expansion into new markets.
3. **Generational relevance:** The bank's 100+ year heritage, while a stability signal, risks being perceived as "outdated and old-fashioned" by younger demographics — particularly Millennials and Gen Z, who are more willing to switch banks and who value community involvement and digital access.
4. **Internal culture unification:** Leadership and FMS both view a clearly articulated brand as a mechanism to resolve the post-merger cultural fragmentation and unite employees under a single mission.

### Growth Strategy
- Pursue expansion **beyond Tomahawk and Merrill** into new geographic markets
- Leverage strong capital position (mutual structure enables capital retention) for potential **acquisitions**
- Build brand awareness among **younger, digitally oriented consumers** while preserving relationship-banking strengths
- Develop **mortgage lending** as a growth platform, given the bank's existing market leadership and loan servicing retention model
- Leverage **mutual status** as a marketing differentiator — currently underutilized; customers and prospects are largely unaware the bank is depositor-owned

### Target Segments
- **Retail banking:** Broad community base; specific opportunity with Millennials and younger generations who value community involvement and local relationships
- **Business banking / commercial:** Established strength, particularly in Merrill market
- **Mortgage:** Primary strength, especially in Tomahawk/northern Wisconsin tourism corridor; working relationships with builders and realtors
- **Community organizations and nonprofits:** Bank is a known civic partner and fundraising supporter

## Competitive Positioning & Differentiation

The bank's competitive differentiation rests on several pillars that are confirmed by both internal and external research participants:

| Differentiator | Evidence from Research |
|---|---|
| **Community involvement** | Noncustomers independently cited the bank's charitable giving and civic presence as its most visible trait |
| **People / relationship quality** | Customers and noncustomers described staff as "fantastic," going beyond transactional roles |
| **Local decision-making** | Customers explicitly said "don't lose that" — citing speed and flexibility versus larger competitors |
| **Mutual ownership structure** | Enables depositor-first focus; no shareholder dividend pressure; largely untold story in the market |
| **Mortgage leadership & servicing retention** | Bank retains loan servicing for the life of the loan — reinforcing relationship continuity; cited as market leader in area |
| **Longevity and financial stability** | 100+ year track record communicated as stability signal; strong capital base |

**Named competitors:** No specific competitor institutions are named in the available report text. However, the research repeatedly contrasts the bank favorably against **"bigger banks"** and **"larger institutions"** that are less flexible, less community-oriented, and less responsive. The competitive threat from both regional/national banks and digital-first challengers is implied but not quantified.

**Market share data:** No specific deposit market share figures are provided in the report.

## Market Dynamics

### Economic Conditions
- Report dated **August 2021** — post-COVID reopening period; the bank is noted as having navigated the pandemic successfully
- Northern Wisconsin's Tomahawk market is influenced by **seasonal tourism and recreation economy**, supporting mortgage and consumer lending activity
- The broader community banking environment is characterized by consolidation pressure, digital disruption, and generational customer shifts

### Threats
- **Generational shift:** Younger consumers may not connect with a 100-year-old brand; risk of being perceived as outdated
- **Digital banking competition:** The bank is investing in technology improvements but is not yet positioned as a digital leader; this gap is a recognized vulnerability
- **Talent and succession:** Imminent wave of retirements among long-tenured staff (many with 20+ year tenures) creates institutional knowledge risk
- **Internal cultural fragmentation:** The unresolved post-merger "us vs. them" dynamic risks becoming visible to customers as integration deepens (e.g., unified phone systems)
- **Competition from larger institutions:** Bigger banks have scale, product breadth, and technology budgets that community banks cannot match directly

### Opportunities
- **Mutual bank differentiation:** The depositor-owned structure is an undermarketed, cause-aligned differentiator as consumers become more values-driven in financial decisions
- **Mortgage expansion:** Tourism-driven northern Wisconsin market and existing builder/realtor relationships create a runway for mortgage growth
- **Geographic expansion and M&A:** Strong capital position gives the bank runway to enter new markets or acquire
- **Millennial and Gen Z capture:** Research (citing Deloitte) suggests younger generations are more likely to switch banks and more receptive to community-oriented institutions that build local relationships
- **Brand unification:** A single, clear brand can resolve internal fragmentation, improve morale, and present a more coherent market identity

## Brand Perception

### Customer Views
- Customers in both Tomahawk and Merrill focus groups expressed **high satisfaction** with personal attention, responsiveness, and the feeling of being known individually
- Specific praised behaviors: staff answering phones directly (no automated routing frustration), proactive account monitoring, in-person flexibility (e.g., going to mobility-limited customers rather than requiring branch visits)
- Customers value **local decision-making and loan turnaround speed** — and explicitly asked the bank to protect this capability
- **Mortgage customers** appreciate that loan servicing is retained in-house throughout the life of the loan — a meaningful trust signal
- Loyalty is high but driven by relationship and service, not brand name — making the bank potentially vulnerable if key relationship-holders retire

### Employee Views
- Employees express genuine pride in the bank's community mission and their personal role in customer relationships
- However, morale is **significantly affected by the unresolved post-merger leadership conflict**: employees describe two opposing management styles, competing policies, and an "us vs. them" atmosphere between Tomahawk and Merrill staff
- Communication is identified as a major internal weakness — employees report feeling uninformed about policy changes, cross-departmental activity, and strategic direction
- Employees want to know the bank's mission and where it is headed; the current lack of clear purpose is described as demotivating despite their personal loyalty to the institution
- Many employees express deep personal connection to the bank's values and community role — a significant cultural asset if unified under a clear brand platform

### Noncustomer Views
- **Awareness of community involvement is high** even among noncustomers — the bank's charitable giving and civic sponsorship penetrate beyond the customer base
- Noncustomers describe the bank's people positively ("their people are fantastic") despite not banking there
- One noncustomer participant noted they could tell staff was "not on the same page" — a concerning signal that internal fragmentation is beginning to surface externally
- The primary barriers to noncustomer conversion are not negative perceptions of the bank but rather inertia, existing banking relationships, or unawareness of the bank's full capabilities

## Key Takeaways for Financial Analysis

### Deposit Base & Ownership Model
- **Mutual charter = depositor-owned:** All capital and earnings are retained; no dividend obligations to outside shareholders. This structural advantage supports a conservative, long-term capital strategy and provides significant flexibility for reinvestment.
- Deposit relationships are **relationship-sticky** — customers stay because of personal connections to specific employees. This creates concentration risk as the bank approaches a significant retirement wave.

### Branch & Expansion Plans
- Current footprint: Tomahawk and Merrill (minimum two locations)
- Leadership has explicitly identified **expansion beyond legacy markets** as a strategic priority
- Strong capital position is cited as enabling potential **acquisitions** — suggesting an M&A-driven growth path alongside organic expansion
- Technology investment is underway to support serving customers "when and where they want" — implying digital channel build-out alongside physical presence

### Mortgage Business
- The bank self-describes as the **leading mortgage lender in its market area**
- **Full servicing retention** model (loan serviced in-house for the life of the mortgage) — generates ongoing customer touchpoints and cross-sell opportunities
- Tomahawk market's tourism economy (second homes, vacation properties, builder activity) provides a differentiated mortgage origination pipeline

### Financial Goals (Inferred)
- Grow market presence and deposits **beyond Tomahawk/Merrill** through brand-driven awareness and potential M&A
- Improve **cross-sell penetration** by leveraging mortgage relationships into full banking relationships
- Develop younger customer segments to **future-proof the deposit base**
- Improve internal efficiency and culture to **reduce the friction cost** of the post-merger dual-culture dynamic

### Rebrand ROI Logic
- The rebrand (to Crossbridge Bank) is positioned as a catalyst for: (1) resolving internal cultural fragmentation, (2) enabling geographic expansion with a scalable brand, (3) attracting younger demographics, and (4) finally telling the untold story of mutual ownership as a values-aligned differentiator
