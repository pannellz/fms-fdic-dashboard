# Wayne Bank — ClientContext Document
**Prepared by Financial Marketing Solutions | Brand Discovery Report Summary | December 2023**

---

## Background & History

Wayne Bank is a community bank headquartered in **Honesdale, Pennsylvania** (Wayne County), founded in **1871** — giving it over 152 years of operating history at the time of this report. The bank has grown primarily through acquisition and now operates **29 branch locations across two states** (Pennsylvania and New York).

The bank is a **publicly traded institution** and operates as the parent brand over three legal brand names currently in market:
- **Wayne Bank** (25 locations — legacy Pennsylvania markets)
- **Bank of Cooperstown** (2 locations — acquired, New York)
- **Bank of the Finger Lakes** (2 locations — acquired, New York, based in **Geneva, NY**)

The acquisitions of Bank of Cooperstown and Bank of the Finger Lakes occurred approximately **three years prior to this report (circa 2020–2021)**. Terms of those acquisitions required that the acquired bank names be retained for a minimum of three years post-close, resulting in a tri-brand structure that has created significant internal and external confusion.

The bank's geographic footprint spans from **Wayne County, PA** eastward into **Lackawanna, Luzerne, and Monroe counties** in Pennsylvania, and northward into **Cooperstown and the Finger Lakes region of New York**. Markets referenced in the report include Honesdale, Clarks Summit, Scranton, Wilkes-Barre, Milford, Tannersville, Effort, Shohola, Wurtsboro, Lakewood, Cooperstown, and Geneva.

---

## Geographic Footprint & Market Characteristics

Wayne Bank's **legacy market** is northeastern Pennsylvania, anchored in rural and small-town communities. These markets are characterized by strong existing brand recognition and deep community roots.

**Expansion markets** — Lackawanna, Luzerne, and Monroe counties in PA, plus the New York markets — represent significantly lower brand awareness. The report explicitly calls out that **deposit market share in Lackawanna, Luzerne, and Monroe counties is weak**, illustrating the awareness deficit outside the legacy footprint.

Key market dynamics include:
- Predominantly **small-town and rural communities** with a values-based customer base that prizes relationships and local service
- Agricultural lending is a specialty in legacy markets
- The New York markets (Cooperstown, Geneva/Finger Lakes) have distinct community identities and a customer base that remains emotionally attached to the acquired bank names
- Noncustomers in Wayne Bank's expansion markets frequently reported **never having heard of Wayne Bank**, even when living in towns where a branch exists

## Organizational Structure & Leadership

The report references **Jim Donnelly** as the current CEO, who is described as having brought significant positive cultural change to the organization. His leadership is credited with generating renewed excitement and momentum internally. Directors and employees across multiple locations cited Donnelly by name as a transformational force.

The bank is a **publicly traded company** with a board of directors. The executive team is based primarily in **Honesdale, PA** (headquarters).

Organizational structure spans:
- Executive team (Honesdale-centric)
- Branch network across PA and NY
- Centralized back-office functions (deposit operations, electronic banking, loan processing)
- Departments: Business Banking, Retail Banking, Residential Lending, Marketing, IT, HR

A critical structural tension noted throughout the report is the **PA vs. NY divide** — employees and customers in the New York markets report feeling underserved, underinvested, and culturally excluded from the Wayne Bank organization. Back-office functions are centralized in Pennsylvania and perceived as slow and inaccessible by NY-based staff.

## Strategic Context

### Why the Rebrand
The rebranding initiative is driven by several converging pressures:

1. **Tri-brand confusion**: Three brand names create operational inefficiency, customer confusion, and internal cultural fragmentation. Employees carry different-branded business cards; loan documents say Wayne Bank while branch signage says Bank of Cooperstown or Bank of the Finger Lakes.
2. **Undefined brand identity**: No employee, executive, director, or customer could consistently articulate what Wayne Bank stands for. Words like "unremarkable," "boring," and "not distinctive" were used.
3. **Logo liability**: The existing Wayne Bank logo mark (a Celtic/friendship knot) is widely perceived as resembling a **swastika**. This concern was raised in *nearly every single meeting* conducted during brand discovery. FMS states unequivocally: "Once you see it, you can't unsee it."
4. **New leadership momentum**: Jim Donnelly's arrival has created internal appetite for change and a window of opportunity to reset culture and brand simultaneously.
5. **Growth ambitions**: The bank seeks to grow beyond legacy markets where awareness is low and where the brand must work harder to win new customers.

### FMS Recommendation on Brand Name
FMS recommends **retaining the Wayne Bank name** and consolidating all 29 locations under a single brand. Rationale includes:
- 25 of 29 locations already carry the Wayne Bank name
- The "Wayne" name is a proper name, not just a geographic identifier, creating a unique brand personality opportunity
- In the Finger Lakes market, the "Finger Lakes" name is used by many other businesses, meaning the bank has no real ownership of that identifier
- Many customers across all three banks stated the **name matters far less than the people and service**

### Growth Strategy & Target Segments
- **Primary deposit driver**: Growing primary checking account relationships, particularly through **direct deposit and bill pay** — currently hampered by a **$12 annual debit card fee** that is widely criticized internally and externally
- **Target demographics**: The report specifically calls out **Gen Z and Millennials** as critical targets, noting their preference for mobile banking, speed, and community involvement
- **Business banking and commercial lending** are growth priorities; treasury management capabilities are cited as inadequate
- **Mortgage and trust services** are product gaps flagged by customers and employees

## Competitive Positioning & Differentiation

### Current Differentiators (Confirmed by Customers)
- **Local decision-making and speed**: Customers chose Wayne Bank specifically because the bank could lend when larger banks could not, and could respond faster. One employee noted being able to charge a higher rate because response speed justified the premium.
- **Relationship banking**: Customers repeatedly cited knowing staff by name, feeling like family, and the emotional connection to specific employees as primary loyalty drivers
- **Agricultural and specialized lending**: Noted as a distinct competency vs. regional banks
- **Longevity and financial stability**: Founded 1871; conservative credit management through multiple recessions cited favorably in the context of 2023 bank failures

### Competitive Weaknesses vs. Competitors
- Smaller community bank competitors are described as having **higher sponsorship visibility** at community events than Wayne Bank
- Technology platforms are behind competitors, particularly in mobile banking, Zelle integration, and online account management
- Marketing spend is described as well below 1% of assets for a ~$2 billion institution — insufficient for a 29-branch, 2-state footprint
- The bank's conservative posture makes it less competitive on deposit products and fee structures

### Named Competitors
No specific competitor institutions are named in the synthesized sections of this report. However, the report references "community bank competitors" that are smaller in size yet maintain greater marketing presence and higher sponsorship levels.

## Market Dynamics

### Economic Conditions
- **2023 bank failures** (Silicon Valley Bank, Signature Bank context implied) are cited as heightening consumer sensitivity to bank stability — a potential opportunity for Wayne Bank to leverage its 152-year history
- **Rising interest rates / inflation** create economic uncertainty in the bank's rural markets
- **Post-pandemic labor market** disruptions have contributed to staffing and retention challenges across the industry, with particular impact on frontline/teller positions

### Threats
- Rapidly rising consumer technology expectations driven by Amazon, Apple, and other non-banking brands are setting a bar that community banks struggle to match
- **Digital-first banking** eliminates the need for physical branch presence, making brand awareness and digital user experience more critical
- Continued **industry consolidation** is referenced as an ongoing threat/context
- **Generational shift**: Millennials and Gen Z are more likely to switch banks and prioritize mobile features; Wayne Bank's current technology and product set is not competitive for these demographics

### Opportunities
- Underpenetrated expansion markets (Lackawanna, Luzerne, Monroe counties in PA; New York markets) offer significant deposit and loan growth potential once brand awareness is established
- The rebrand itself is a catalyst to unify the team, improve culture, and signal to the market that the bank is evolving
- Leveraging 152-year stability narrative in the context of 2023 bank failures
- Cross-selling to existing customer base (currently not marketed to beyond branch visits)

## Brand Perception

### Customer Views
**Wayne Bank customers** are highly loyal, driven almost entirely by **personal relationships with specific staff members**. Customers state they would not leave even for better rates. Common praise: friendliness, responsiveness, being known by name. Common complaints: technology (online banking described as "rough," "unreliable," and "not user-friendly"), the $12 debit card fee, and inconsistent service across branches.

**Bank of Cooperstown customers** express strong attachment to the local brand name but acknowledge the name matters less than the people. Some expressed they would have left if forced to become "just Wayne Bank." Several noted increased employee turnover and reduced community presence since the acquisition.

**Bank of the Finger Lakes customers** have experienced frustration during the transition. One customer noted being on their 4th loan officer in 5 years. The "takeover" language is used. Customers observe declining community involvement since the acquisition.

### Employee Views
Employees are split along geographic/brand lines:
- **Wayne Bank (PA) employees**: Generally more positive but frustrated by inefficiencies, lack of training, inadequate compensation, short staffing, and poor communication
- **Bank of Cooperstown and Bank of the Finger Lakes (NY) employees**: Significantly more negative, feeling excluded, undervalued, and treated as second-class within the organization. Direct quote: *"Just because we're up here in NY doesn't mean we don't exist or matter."*

Widespread employee sentiment is that the bank's purpose is perceived to be **shareholder return**, not community service — a brand-damaging internal narrative. Employees want to believe in a higher purpose.

### Noncustomer Views
Noncustomers frequently **had never heard of Wayne Bank**, even when living in towns with a branch. The name "Wayne Bank" is assumed by many to be geographically limited to Wayne County. Noncustomers who were aware of the bank described it as conservative, old-fashioned, and not distinctive. Several said better advertising would make them consider the bank.

## Key Takeaways for Financial Analysis

### Deposit Drivers & Barriers
- **$12 annual debit card fee** is the single most-cited product barrier. It discourages debit card issuance, reducing transaction fee income and limiting the bank's ability to capture primary checking relationships (direct deposit, bill pay). Eliminating or restructuring this fee is likely a near-term deposit growth lever.
- **Primary checking account capture** is the stated strategic priority for low-cost deposit growth
- **Treasury management capabilities are inadequate** — one employee described winning a large commercial relationship and "messing it up every way we can" due to product limitations (sweep accounts, money markets)
- Technology deficiencies are driving customers to use an average of **2.80 banks** — Wayne Bank is not the primary institution for many of its own customers

### Branch Plans
- The bank operates **29 locations** and the report implies no near-term branch reduction strategy; the focus is on unifying all 29 under the Wayne Bank brand
- The New York markets (4 locations) have been under-invested relative to PA markets — resource parity is an implied need
- Old signage (legacy logos, old brand elements) remains in multiple locations including Effort and Tannersville, indicating deferred physical plant investment

### Financial Goals & Scale
- The bank is described as approximately **$2 billion in assets**
- Marketing budget is cited as significantly below 1% of assets — an employee noted this as a recruitment barrier when bringing on a business development officer
- The bank is positioned as a **top-performing community bank** with consistently strong performance ratios and conservative credit culture
- Growth through acquisition has been a stated strategic pillar; the cultural and operational integration challenges documented here suggest future acquisitions will require more deliberate onboarding and brand integration protocols
- The rebrand and strategic marketing plan are positioned as enablers of **organic growth in underpenetrated markets**, particularly in expansion counties where deposit market share is low
