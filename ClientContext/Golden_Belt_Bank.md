# ClientContext: Golden Belt Bank
### Brand Discovery Report — FMS, August 2019

---

## Background & History

Golden Belt Bank was founded in **1920** in **Hays, Kansas**, giving it a nearly 100-year operating history at the time of this report. The bank is headquartered in the **Hays/Ellis, Kansas** market (Ellis County) and has recently expanded into **Fort Morgan, Colorado** through the acquisition of **Morgan Federal Bank**. The bank is approaching its centennial anniversary, which represents both a branding milestone and a strategic inflection point.

The acquisition of Morgan Federal Bank introduced the bank's second operating market but also created significant operational, cultural, and brand fragmentation challenges (detailed below). At the time of this report, the Colorado location was still operating under the dual name **"Morgan Federal Bank, a branch of Golden Belt Bank,"** which created marketplace confusion and was a primary driver of the brand discovery engagement with FMS.

---

## Geographic Footprint & Market Characteristics

- **Primary markets:** Hays, KS; Ellis, KS (Ellis County)
- **Expansion market:** Fort Morgan, CO (acquired via Morgan Federal Bank)
- All markets are characterized as **very rural**, with tight-knit communities where personal relationships and word-of-mouth carry significant weight
- **Data privacy and confidentiality** are culturally important to customers in both Kansas and Colorado markets — noted as a distinct regional trait
- Despite nearly 100 years in Hays and Ellis, brand awareness among residents (including college-age consumers) was **surprisingly low**, even within the home market
- Fort Morgan awareness of the Golden Belt Bank name was even more limited, with the Morgan Federal name carrying more local equity — though that equity was being eroded by post-acquisition changes

---

## Organizational Structure & Leadership

- The bank has an **executive leadership team** based in Hays/Ellis described as experienced, collaborative, and values-driven, with a functioning **succession plan** already in place
- Leadership is characterized internally as exhibiting **integrity, work ethic, and genuine concern for employees and community**
- A **board of directors** participates in the bank's strategic direction (directors completed written brand surveys as part of this research)
- Dedicated internal teams identified include: Mortgage/Business Banking, Marketing, IT/Social/Digital, and HR
- **Fort Morgan lacks a designated local leader** — a critical gap unanimously identified by Colorado employees. No clear point of authority exists at that branch, which undermines customer confidence and employee morale
- The bank is navigating a **generational shift in human resources**, with many long-tenured employees aging toward retirement and a need to attract and develop younger talent

---

## Strategic Context

### Why the Rebrand
The engagement was initiated to address several converging pressures:
1. **Post-acquisition confusion** from the dual-name situation (Golden Belt Bank vs. Morgan Federal Bank)
2. **Low brand awareness** — even in the primary Kansas market, many consumers were unfamiliar with the bank despite its 99-year history
3. **Outdated visual identity** — the current type-only logo (no mark/icon) was broadly described as "generic," "forgettable," "boring," and leading noncustomers to assume the bank lacked modern digital capabilities ("Looking at it, you assume they don't have mobile banking or an app")
4. **Internal fragmentation** — morale issues in Fort Morgan and some employee dissatisfaction in Kansas required a unifying brand narrative

### Name Strategy Recommendation
FMS evaluated three options and recommended **Option 3: unify all locations under the Golden Belt Bank name**, retiring the Morgan Federal Bank identity. Rationale:
- Golden Belt Bank carries ~100 years of equity in Kansas
- The dual-name approach has already caused customer/employee attrition and confusion in Fort Morgan
- JD Power data cited: **brand awareness is the #1 reason consumers choose a bank** after deciding to switch
- Kahneman's research cited: familiarity is translated as "right" in decision-making **more than 70% of the time**

### Growth Strategy
- The bank is explicitly positioned for **continued organic growth and future acquisitions**, with its financial strength cited as the foundation
- Leadership has articulated a vision of Golden Belt Bank as a **"lifetime financial partner"** — serving customers from college through retirement, home ownership, and business formation
- The emerging brand narrative centers on: *"a 100-year-old bank with the energy of a fresh brand"* — leveraging stability and wisdom while projecting relevance and forward momentum

### Target Segments
- **Individual retail customers** (primary): personal relationships, home loans, life-stage banking
- **Business banking and mortgage** customers (growth segment — dedicated focus group conducted)
- **College students and young adults** — explicitly identified as an awareness gap; Hays employees noted that college students in the market had no knowledge of the bank
- **Millennials/Gen Y** — cited via Deloitte research as a priority segment for community banks given their willingness to switch institutions and their valuation of local relationships

---

## Competitive Positioning & Differentiation

Golden Belt Bank's core differentiation rests on **relationship-driven community banking** — a model that is explicitly contrasted against larger regional and national banks. Key differentiators identified across all research groups:

- **Local mortgage decision-making**: Loans are decided locally (not sent to a distant credit center), making the bank faster and more flexible than competitors
- **Loan servicing retained in-house**: Reinforces the relationship model; customers deal with the same people throughout the life of a loan
- **Know-you-by-name service culture**: Multiple customers cited staff knowing their names before they signed in
- **Long-tenured employees**: Employee longevity was cited by noncustomers as a signal of institutional stability and employee satisfaction
- **Security posture**: Described internally as "10 levels above other banks" in IT security — a meaningful differentiator in rural markets where data privacy concerns are elevated

**Competitive vulnerabilities** (gaps vs. competitors):
- The outdated visual identity projects a lack of digital sophistication, leading noncustomers to assume the bank is behind on technology
- **No logo mark** — difficult to build recognition in digital/mobile channels
- Fort Morgan morale and service degradation have likely ceded competitive ground to other local lenders in that market

## Market Dynamics

### Economic Conditions
- No specific macroeconomic data provided in the report; however, the bank's **conservative credit standards and quality credit control** are cited as having produced stable, high-performing financial results through economic cycles
- Rural market dynamics are implied throughout — limited population growth, tight talent pools, and high community visibility for any bank missteps

### Threats
- **Generational workforce transition**: Long-tenured employees aging out; replacing institutional knowledge and relationship equity is an acute risk
- **Digital expectations gap**: Noncustomers are pre-judging the bank's capabilities based on visual identity, creating a barrier to acquisition before any interaction occurs
- **Fort Morgan competitive exposure**: Customer and employee attrition following the acquisition has weakened the bank's position in the Colorado market; competitors may have captured displaced relationships
- **Low brand awareness**: Even in the home market, insufficient awareness limits organic growth potential

### Opportunities
- **Centennial anniversary** (~2020): A significant marketing and storytelling moment to establish brand relevance and amplify awareness
- **Community bank positioning vs. large banks**: Deloitte research cited in the report notes community banks' relationship model as a **decisive competitive advantage** with Millennials
- **Mortgage leadership**: The bank's strong reputation in home lending is a platform for deepening and expanding customer relationships
- **Business banking growth**: Identified as a target segment with dedicated research focus
- **Future acquisitions**: Leadership has signaled appetite for additional acquisitions, with financial strength cited as enabling this strategy

## Brand Perception

### Customer Views (Golden Belt Bank — Kansas)
- Overwhelmingly positive toward **people and service**: staff friendliness, name recognition, going "above and beyond"
- Strong loyalty driven by personal relationships, not products or rates
- Mortgage/home lending reputation is strong and well-known
- Security and data privacy perceived as a strength
- Representative quotes:
  - *"The people here are at the highest level. You can recommend them and know your referral will get good service."*
  - *"There is always a friendly cheerful face calling me by name. It amazes me that they know who I am before I even sign a slip."*

### Customer Views (Morgan Federal / Fort Morgan — Colorado)
- Customers are aware that Morgan Federal Bank has fundamentally changed despite the name remaining on the building
- Reduced staffing ("skeleton crew") and product/service changes have eroded trust and satisfaction
- Customer attrition is noted as a measurable outcome of the post-acquisition experience
- Significant confusion about who the bank actually is and who to speak with when problems arise

### Employee Views (Golden Belt Bank — Kansas)
- Generally positive about the bank as a workplace and proud of its service culture
- Some dissatisfaction around **perceived inequities in workload, compensation, and recognition**
- Aware of the low brand awareness problem — one employee noted that college students in Hays don't know where the bank is located
- Quote: *"Customer service is first and foremost on everyone's mind. We want to make the customer happy."*

### Employee Views (Morgan Federal — Fort Morgan)
- **Severely demoralized** — described as feeling like "step kids" within the organization
- Have lost colleagues, access to customer information, product offerings, and decision-making authority
- Feel their skills and experience are underutilized due to system/policy restrictions post-conversion
- No designated local leader — employees have no authority figure to direct customers to
- Quote: *"It's crazy when we have lost so many customers and so many employees, shouldn't something change?"*

### Noncustomer Views (Hays and Fort Morgan)
- **Low awareness** of Golden Belt Bank even in Hays — a striking finding given the bank's 99-year history
- Those unfamiliar with the bank made **negative pre-judgments** based solely on the current visual identity (generic, outdated, no digital capability assumed)
- Those who became aware of employee longevity responded positively: *"How long the people have worked there is incredibly impressive. It shows me security."*
- Mortgage reputation had some reach even among noncustomers: *"They are excellent with customer service with their mortgages."*
- Security protocols resonated positively when described: *"Knowing they have those security protocols in place makes me feel good."*

## Key Takeaways for Financial Analysis

### Deposit & Revenue Drivers
- **Home mortgage lending** is the bank's primary product strength and reputational anchor — local decision-making and loan servicing retention are competitive advantages that support both customer acquisition and retention
- **Relationship banking model** drives deposit stickiness — customers stay because of people, not rates, which is favorable for deposit cost management
- **Business banking** is an identified growth segment but appears underdeveloped relative to the mortgage business — represents a meaningful incremental revenue opportunity

### Branch & Market Considerations
- **Fort Morgan branch is underperforming** and experiencing both customer and employee attrition — financial metrics at that branch are likely deteriorating. A leadership hire and name unification are identified as the most urgent remediation steps
- The bank has signaled **appetite for additional acquisitions** beyond Fort Morgan — the brand infrastructure investment (unified name, new identity, brand standards) is partly designed to enable scalable future M&A integration
- **Hays/Ellis market** has untapped awareness potential despite 99-year presence — organic growth through marketing investment is feasible without requiring new branch infrastructure

### Financial Goals (Implied)
- Sustain and grow **mortgage market share** in existing markets
- **Reverse attrition** in Fort Morgan and stabilize that branch's financial contribution
- Build brand infrastructure to support **future acquisitions** as a growth vehicle
- Attract **younger customers** (college students, Millennials) to extend the customer lifecycle and reduce average customer age — critical for long-term deposit and loan growth
- The centennial anniversary (~2020) is a near-term catalyst for a brand relaunch marketing campaign

### Brand Investment Rationale
- The bank is preparing to invest in a **full brand identity system** (new logo mark, typography, color palette, brand standards) plus a **strategic marketing plan**
- This investment is framed as a prerequisite for competitive survival — not discretionary — given that noncustomers are pre-disqualifying the bank based on its current appearance before any service interaction occurs
