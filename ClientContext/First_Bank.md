# ClientContext: First Bank — Brand Discovery Report Summary
**Prepared by:** Financial Marketing Solutions (FMS)
**Report Date:** March 2022

---

## Background & History

First Bank is a community bank serving **Southeast Oklahoma (SEOK)** with a history of **more than 120 years**. The bank is described as a financial and civic pillar in every community it serves, with deep roots in relationship banking, honest dealings, and community investment.

A significant recent event is a **merger with First Bank and Trust**, which accelerated the bank's geographic expansion but also introduced internal brand confusion. Leadership acknowledged that rapid growth — combined with the lack of a clearly defined brand — has created fragmentation internally and reduced brand clarity externally. The report was commissioned in early 2022 to diagnose brand gaps and set the stage for a formal rebrand and strategic marketing initiative.

Over the past decade, First Bank **more than doubled its branch footprint from 4 to 9 locations** through both organic growth and the merger.

---

## Geographic Footprint & Market Characteristics

First Bank operates **9 branch locations across Southeast Oklahoma**, covering a corridor from **Idabel, Broken Bow, and Hochatown** in the east to **Atoka and Coalgate** in the west. Additional confirmed markets include **Antlers**.

The bank's markets are characterized by:
- **Small, rural towns** that are geographically spread out
- **Limited population transfer/in-migration**, making new customer acquisition structurally more difficult
- **Generational banking loyalty** — customers often bank where their parents and grandparents banked, creating high switching inertia
- A regional culture that values **local ownership, community support, and personal relationships**

Customer and noncustomer focus groups were conducted in **Antlers, Atoka, and Broken Bow**.

## Organizational Structure & Leadership

The report references several leadership tiers without naming specific individuals:
- **Executive team** (participated in focus groups and written surveys)
- **Board of Directors** (written survey participants)
- **Location Managers** (branch-level leaders, separate focus group)
- **Loan Committee** (separate focus group)
- **Marketing and IT departments** (separate focus groups and surveys)

One executive was quoted describing the bank's mission as: *"We are servants serving to create a better SEOK for everyone."*

The bank is described as **family-owned**, which directly supports local decision-making authority. Leadership is aware of internal culture and communication deficiencies and is actively seeking to address them through the rebranding process.

## Strategic Context

### Why the Rebrand
The merger with First Bank and Trust, combined with rapid branch expansion, produced a brand identity that became **"less clear and less known, both internally and externally."** Employees lacked a consistent understanding of the bank's vision and purpose; customers in newer markets lacked the 100+ year context that anchored the brand in legacy communities.

### Growth Strategy
- Expand into **new markets beyond legacy footprint** while reinforcing presence in existing communities
- Invest in **technology, digital products, and delivery channels** to serve customers on their own terms
- Build a **scalable brand model** that can travel with the bank as it enters additional towns in the SEOK region
- Continue organic growth supplemented by potential future M&A

### Target Segments
- **Youth/Students:** iStudent Checking account is specifically designed to break generational banking loyalty cycles — acquiring children of competitor customers (notably **Ameristate Bank** in Atoka) and converting them into long-term customers. Program rewards good grades with account incentives.
- **Young adults:** Converted from student accounts into loan customers
- **Small businesses and local entrepreneurs:** Beneficiaries of local decision-making and flexible credit underwriting
- **Underserved credit borrowers:** The bank explicitly serves people and businesses that larger banks have exited or declined

## Competitive Positioning & Differentiation

### Named Competitor
- **Ameristate Bank** — identified specifically in Atoka as a primary competitor with deep generational loyalty among local families. The iStudent Checking program is cited as successfully breaking Ameristate's generational hold.

### Key Differentiators
1. **Local decision-making speed:** Loan decisions within **30 minutes** for requests within a lender's authority, versus 2–4 days at competitors. A real estate attorney (noncustomer) noted that First Bank loan closings can be completed in **half a day**, versus much longer timelines at other institutions.
2. **Community investment depth:** Described by customers, noncustomers, and employees as unmatched in the region. Notable examples include covering **hospital payroll** to prevent a community hospital from closing, funding school programs, and placing staff on school boards, city councils, and hospital boards.
3. **Branch scale:** 9 locations across SEOK — more than any direct competitor in the region.
4. **Product innovation:** First Bank is described as ahead of peers its size in new product adoption, including DocuSign, personalized debit cards, card valet, mobile deposit, and iStudent Checking.
5. **Flexible credit access:** Serves customers and businesses that larger regional banks have declined or abandoned — described as "a source of credit not available to most people in the area."
6. **Direct banker access:** Customers can call their banker directly; bankers proactively monitor accounts and alert customers to suspicious activity.

### Brand Positioning Statement (Emerging)
*"Southeast Oklahoma's true community bank"* — active, involved, flexible, responsive, honest, and relationship-driven. Positioned to grow without losing its community heart.

## Market Dynamics

### Economic Conditions & Context
- Southeast Oklahoma is a rural, small-town economy with limited population transfer, meaning organic household growth is slow
- Many markets have seen larger banks exit or reduce presence, creating opportunity for First Bank to serve as the dominant local institution
- Hospital viability is cited as a community health indicator — one executive noted *"if the hospital goes, the community goes"*
- The region is experiencing the same **generational shift** affecting all community banks, with Millennials/Gen Y more willing to switch financial institutions than prior generations but also more values-driven

### Key Threats
- **Generational loyalty to legacy competitors** (especially Ameristate in Atoka)
- **Employee retention and generational workforce transitions** — the bank is navigating leadership succession and staff turnover
- **Technology expectations** — rapid evolution of digital banking creating pressure to continuously invest
- **Internal fragmentation** post-merger — risk of brand dilution if culture and communication are not addressed
- **Relevance gap** — risk of losing younger demographics if brand messaging doesn't evolve

### Key Opportunities
- **Breaking generational banking ties** through youth-targeted products
- **Newer markets** where brand awareness is low — opportunity to establish First Bank as the community bank of choice without legacy competitor dominance
- **Promoting existing products more effectively** — product awareness is lagging product availability
- **Digital/online channel growth** — stated goal to allow customers to bank "when, where, and how they want"
- **Hochatown/tourism economy** — Broken Bow/Hochatown area is a notable regional tourism destination, which may support small business and hospitality lending

## Brand Perception

### Customer Views
- Customers are **highly loyal** and credit the bank's people and relationships as primary reasons for staying
- Specific praise for: being known by name, direct banker access, proactive fraud monitoring, quick loan decisions, and genuine care
- Customers acknowledge First Bank as the **most community-invested bank in the region** — several cited it as a reason they bank there
- The "In It To Win It" contest and iStudent Checking campaigns have **extremely high unaided recall** among customers
- Some customers desire **more proactive communication** on new products and bank news

### Employee Views
- Employees express **pride** in their work and in the bank's community impact
- Widespread frustration with **top-down communication failures** — policy changes not communicated in advance, employees learning of changes only after being written up for non-compliance
- Employees feel **underappreciated and unacknowledged** by upper management — specifically: limited recognition, lack of encouragement, and absence of executive visibility at the branch level
- Employees are enthusiastic about product innovation and want to be **empowered ambassadors** of the brand
- Communication style issues: messages delivered via email when phone or in-person would be more appropriate for the content

### Noncustomer Views
- Even non-customers have a **positive perception** of First Bank's community involvement and people quality
- Noncustomers recognize First Bank's **billboard marketing featuring local students** — high brand recall from this campaign
- The branch network expansion is noticed and viewed positively ("you see them everywhere")
- Noncustomers confirm First Bank's loan closing speed as superior to competitors

## Key Takeaways for Financial Analysis

### Deposit & Revenue Drivers
- **iStudent Checking** is an active deposit acquisition strategy targeting a demographic that competitors have not effectively reached; success in Atoka documented — breaking Ameristate's generational hold and converting students into loan customers as young adults
- **Relationship-driven retention** is the primary mechanism for low deposit attrition in legacy markets — the bank benefits from long-standing, emotionally loyal customer relationships
- **Community sponsorship and visibility** functions as a low-cost, high-impact brand acquisition channel
- Product breadth (digital tools, personalized products) supports customer stickiness and cross-sell potential

### Branch Plans
- Current footprint: **9 locations** across SEOK (up from 4 a decade ago)
- Leadership signals readiness for **continued geographic expansion** into additional SEOK towns
- The rebrand is explicitly designed to make the bank's model **more scalable** into new markets where there is no legacy brand equity
- Newer markets (e.g., post-merger locations) are underperforming on brand awareness relative to legacy markets — targeted marketing investment in these locations is expected

### Financial & Strategic Goals (Implied)
- Grow market share across SEOK, with particular emphasis on **new and emerging markets**
- Expand the **youth-to-adult customer pipeline** via iStudent Checking → loan conversion
- Increase **product adoption rates** through improved internal and external communication
- Build a brand infrastructure that supports **M&A scalability** — future acquisitions or de novo entries are anticipated
- Improve **employee retention** to protect the quality of service that drives customer loyalty and differentiation
- Position the bank for *"the next 100 years"* — language used by leadership suggesting a long-term, generational ownership and stewardship mindset
