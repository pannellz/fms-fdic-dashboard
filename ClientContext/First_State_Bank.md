# First State Bank — ClientContext Document
**Prepared by FMS | Brand Discovery Report Summary | February 2020**

---

## Background & History

First State Bank is a community bank headquartered in **rural southwestern Ohio**, founded in **1884** — giving it over 135 years of operating history at the time of this report. The bank was built on a foundation of **faith, family, service, and relationships** and has served rural communities throughout its entire history. Its brand identity in legacy markets is strongly associated with trust, stability, and deep community embeddedness. The bank operates **13 locations** across its market footprint.

The bank has a **century-plus track record of consistent financial growth**, managed conservatively with disciplined credit standards. Leadership describes profitability as foundational: *"If we don't make money, we can't serve anybody."* The institution is described as financially sound and well-managed, though specific asset size, deposit totals, or capital ratios are not disclosed in this report.

---

## Geographic Footprint & Market Characteristics

- **Legacy markets:** Rural southwestern Ohio communities where the bank is deeply embedded, well-known, and highly trusted. Strong brand equity, name recognition, and loyal customer base in these areas.
- **Growth markets (newer/northern markets):** Expanded geographic footprint where brand equity has **not translated effectively**. These markets have greater competition, lower awareness, a more dispersed workforce, and inconsistent brand communication.
- **13 branch locations** total across the footprint.
- Growth markets are described as having **more job opportunities and a more competitive labor market**, making staffing and retention more challenging than in legacy rural markets.
- The bank's markets are characterized by a **"pride in small-town, rural way of life"** — a shared value system between the bank, its employees, and its customers that is a meaningful differentiator.

---

## Organizational Structure & Leadership

- The **executive team** is described as experienced, collaborative, stable, and values-driven. Leadership is highlighted as the bank's single greatest internal strength across all respondent groups.
- The **president** is noted by name recognition among employees — described as someone who "says hello by name to every single one of us and cares about our families."
- **Board of directors** are described as "approachable, down-to-earth, humble people that live everyday normal lives very similar to their employees."
- Internal governance structures include a **Customer Services Committee** and a **Digital Services Review team**, both focused on identifying product/service gaps and technology opportunities.
- A **new mentor program and training facilitator** had recently been introduced at the time of the report, signaling leadership awareness of training gaps.
- The bank operates across **multiple functional departments** captured in focus groups: Retail Banking, Business/Commercial Banking, Mortgage, Marketing/Digital, IT, HR, and executive leadership.

## Strategic Context

### Why the Rebrand
The primary catalyst for the brand initiative is **geographic expansion into growth markets where legacy brand equity has not transferred**. As the bank has grown from its rural southwestern Ohio roots northward into more competitive markets, it faces:
- Lower brand awareness among prospective customers
- Inconsistent brand messaging and customer experience across locations
- A more geographically dispersed workforce lacking unified cultural identity
- A brand and visual identity that no longer reflects the bank's evolving scale and ambition

The rebrand is also timed to coincide with what leadership describes as a pivotal moment — the bank is "positioning itself for another century of success" and wants the brand to reflect both its heritage and its forward momentum.

### Growth Strategy
- **Geographic expansion** into northern Ohio markets with greater competition and population density
- **Product and technology investment** to remain competitive with regional and national banks
- **Internal unification** — creating a consistent brand culture and customer experience across all 13 locations
- **Talent investment** — addressing compensation, training, and retention to support growth
- Leadership explicitly understands that "intentional growth requires additional investment into products, services, resources and technology"

### Target Segments
- **Retail/consumer banking** customers in rural and small-town Ohio communities
- **Business/commercial banking** clients — a distinct focus group segment, suggesting commercial banking is a meaningful revenue line
- **Mortgage customers** — also a distinct segment with its own focus group
- **Millennials/Gen Y** — referenced in the report as a priority future segment; the bank recognizes this generation is more willing to switch financial institutions and values community involvement, making FSB's community brand potentially resonant if properly communicated
- **Noncustomers in growth markets** — identified as a key acquisition opportunity; noncustomers have positive impressions of FSB's community involvement but limited awareness of its value proposition

## Competitive Positioning & Differentiation

### Key Differentiators
1. **Relationship banking depth:** Customers are known by name; bankers know families. Described by customers as unmatched vs. competitors: *"The other banking centers don't give you the individualized attention that First State Bank gives."*
2. **Community involvement:** Universally recognized — even by noncustomers who knew little else about the bank. Visible at county fairs, charity events, and community sponsorships. Described as a *"true community bank"* in markets where competitors have reduced their local presence.
3. **Shared rural values:** A rare alignment between the bank's values and those of its customer base — pride in small-town life, local ownership mentality.
4. **Financially conservative, stable institution:** 100+ years without failure, consistent profitability, conservative credit culture — a trust signal in rural markets.
5. **People:** Employees are described as friendly, caring, and trustworthy. Noncustomers note: *"There is a human side to First State Bank that we don't have with these other banks."*

### Named Competitors
- **Fifth Third Bank** — cited explicitly as a competitor offering higher compensation that lures away FSB-trained employees. Likely a primary competitive threat in growth/northern markets.
- **Larger regional and national banks** — referenced generically as the comparison point for technology, digital experience, and compensation benchmarks.
- The report notes that customers on average **use three banks**, meaning FSB competes for wallet share even among existing customers.

### Competitive Vulnerabilities
- **Technology gap:** Mobile app, online banking platform, and debit card reliability lag behind larger competitors — to the point where local businesses have posted signs refusing FSB debit cards.
- **Brand awareness deficit** in growth markets relative to regional/national competitors.
- **Compensation gap** causing talent drain to better-paying competitors.

## Market Dynamics

### Economic & Market Conditions
- Report dated **February 2020** — pre-COVID, in a stable economic environment.
- Rural southwestern Ohio markets are characterized by slower growth, tight-knit communities, and limited competition — favorable conditions for community banking.
- Northern/growth markets are more economically dynamic with greater job competition, more banking options, and a more transient workforce.

### Threats
- **Digital disruption:** Customer expectations for digital banking experiences have been elevated by Amazon-level UX standards; community banks face structural disadvantage in technology investment.
- **Talent competition:** Larger banks (specifically Fifth Third) offering higher pay are recruiting FSB-trained staff, creating a costly training-and-turnover cycle.
- **Brand invisibility in growth markets:** Without a coherent brand story, FSB risks remaining an unknown quantity to prospective customers in newer markets.
- **Debit card/technology reliability failures:** Active reputational damage occurring at the local merchant level.

### Opportunities
- **Millennials:** Identified as more open to switching banks and valuing community involvement — FSB's core strengths directly align with this segment's stated preferences.
- **Competitor retreat from rural markets:** Other banks have reduced community presence, leaving FSB as the most visible local banking institution in some markets.
- **Unified brand platform:** A coherent brand story told consistently across all 13 locations could amplify existing goodwill and convert noncustomer awareness into new relationships.
- **Cross-sell potential:** Employees currently know only ~43% of bank products (per ABA benchmark data cited in report); improved training could meaningfully increase revenue per customer.

## Brand Perception

### Customer Views
- **Strongly positive on service and relationships.** Customers feel known, valued, and treated equally regardless of account size: *"I feel important. They treat you the same if you have $5 or $5 million."*
- **Appreciate community presence** — visibility at local events reinforces trust.
- **Critical of technology** — online banking, mobile app functionality, and debit card reliability are consistent pain points.
- **Want more proactive communication** from the bank about products, services, and account issues.

### Employee Views
- **Pride in leadership and culture** — the executive team's values and accessibility are a genuine source of employee loyalty and motivation.
- **Frustrated by operational gaps:** Communication, training, staffing levels, and technology are the four most cited internal challenges.
- **Compensation is a retention crisis:** Multiple employees describe a pattern of FSB training staff who then leave for better-paying institutions (Fifth Third cited). *"We train people and then they leave for Fifth-Third because they are paid more."*
- **Feel disconnected from brand purpose:** When asked what leadership most wants employees to understand about the bank, employees reported a telling silence — they didn't have an answer.
- **Spread too thin:** Staffing levels have not kept pace with the bank's growth, creating burnout and service delivery concerns.

### Noncustomer Views
- **Aware of community involvement** even without being customers — FSB's event presence and charitable activity has created positive ambient awareness.
- **Positive impressions of the bank's people:** Noncustomers describe FSB employees as "amazing people" and note the human element distinguishes FSB from larger banks.
- **Low awareness of FSB's specific value proposition** — noncustomers want to hear more about what makes FSB a compelling banking choice.
- **Interested in the relationship model:** The community bank experience resonates as a concept even among those who haven't yet chosen FSB.

## Key Takeaways for Financial Analysis

### Deposit & Revenue Drivers
- **Core deposit base is relationship-driven** and concentrated in legacy rural markets where customer loyalty is high and attrition is low.
- **Multi-bank customer behavior** (average of 3 banks per customer) suggests significant **wallet share opportunity** — cross-selling and deepening existing relationships is a near-term revenue lever.
- **Commercial/business banking** is a distinct segment with its own focus group, suggesting meaningful contribution to the loan and deposit portfolio.
- **Mortgage** is a distinct product line being actively managed and researched.
- Technology friction (transfer delays of 3–10 days between banks, inability to pay loans at branch without an account) may be suppressing deposit consolidation and product adoption.

### Branch & Operational Plans
- **13 current locations** across southwestern Ohio into northern growth markets.
- No specific new branch opening dates or locations named, but the growth strategy implies continued geographic expansion northward.
- Back-office staffing has **not scaled with branch growth** — a constraint on operational capacity and service delivery.
- The bank is investing in an **intranet platform** to improve cross-location communication and brand consistency.

### Financial Goals & Strategic Priorities (as expressed by leadership)
1. Achieve a refreshed brand identity that works across all markets, legacy and growth
2. Invest in technology to close the digital experience gap with regional competitors
3. Address compensation and benefits to reduce turnover and protect the talent pipeline
4. Build structured training programs to improve cross-sell capability and employee confidence
5. Improve internal communication infrastructure to create brand consistency across 13 locations
6. Position the bank for **"another 100 years of growth and success"** — language suggesting a long-term, generational institutional vision rather than a near-term exit or acquisition strategy
