# Security National Bank — ClientContext Document
**Prepared by FMS (Financial Marketing Solutions) | March 2018 Brand Discovery Report**

---

## Background & History

Security National Bank (SNB) was **founded in 1884**, making it approximately **134 years old** at the time of this report. The bank is headquartered in **Sioux City, Iowa** (referred to throughout as the "legacy market"), where it holds a dominant position as a leading financial institution and acknowledged community pillar. The Sioux City market is characterized by deep brand familiarity and a loyal multi-generational customer base — in some cases, families with three generations banking at SNB.

The bank has maintained **strong capital ratios** and has consistently posted **high-performing financial numbers relative to peer banks**, attributed to conservative credit management and disciplined underwriting standards. Employee tenure is notably high, with an **average employee longevity of approximately 13 years**, contributing to relationship continuity with customers. SNB has demonstrated resilience through multiple economic cycles due to its conservative management philosophy.

---

## Geographic Footprint & Market Characteristics

SNB operates across **two primary markets**:

- **Sioux City, Iowa (Legacy Market):** The bank's home market, where it commands strong brand awareness, deep community roots, and a loyal but **aging customer base**. Brand recognition is high; the SNB name carries substantial equity. The bank is regarded as a "market leader" in Sioux City.

- **Sioux Falls, South Dakota (Growth Market):** SNB has been present in Sioux Falls for approximately **10 years** at the time of the report. Brand awareness is materially lower here. The market presents a complication: there was a former "Security Bank" that no longer exists, and a new "Security Bank" is simultaneously entering the Sioux Falls market, creating potential consumer confusion that SNB must actively manage through marketing investment and differentiation.

The broader service area is referred to as the **"Siouxland region."** SNB's strategic intent is to deepen its penetration in Sioux Falls while protecting its legacy position in Sioux City.

## Organizational Structure & Leadership

The report describes a **stable, experienced, and respected leadership team** with significant tenure and continuity through leadership transitions. Executives are characterized internally and externally by:
- Integrity and doing "the right thing"
- Genuine concern for employees
- Conservative, prudent decision-making
- Long-term commitment to the organization

No individual executive names are cited in the summarized sections of the report. The bank appears to operate with **separate charters** for Sioux City and Sioux Falls entities, though they operate under a unified SNB brand. Focus groups were conducted with distinct employee groups including: **Executives, Sioux City Employees, Sioux Falls Employees, Commercial/Ag Banking, Mortgage, Wealth Management, and IT/Social/Digital** teams — indicating a multi-division operational structure.

## Strategic Context

### Why the Rebrand?
The primary driver of the brand discovery and subsequent rebrand initiative is **relevance and growth**. The report identifies several converging pressures:

1. **Aging customer base** — SNB's core Sioux City customers are loyal but older. Executives and employees explicitly recognize the need to reduce the average customer age.
2. **Expansion into Sioux Falls** — Growth in a new market requires heightened brand awareness and marketing investment where SNB has limited equity.
3. **Competitive and demographic shifts** — The rise of the next generation of banking customers (Millennials/Gen Y), digital/mobile banking, and changing consumer expectations demand a modernized brand expression.
4. **Outdated visual identity** — Across all research groups (employees, customers, noncustomers), the existing SNB logo was widely described as dated, stale, and not professionally credible — creating a barrier to noncustomer conversion.

### Growth Strategy
- **Organic growth** in both Sioux City and Sioux Falls markets
- **Increased marketing resource allocation** to Sioux Falls to build brand awareness
- **Digital/mobile banking investment** to attract and retain younger customers
- **Logo and identity evolution** (not revolution) to modernize brand perception while preserving 134 years of brand equity in the legacy market
- Coordination of all marketing, advertising, and internal branding under a **unified brand system**

### Target Segments
- **Primary retention focus:** Existing loyal customer base in Sioux City (older, high-trust customers)
- **Primary acquisition targets:** 
  - Noncustomers in Sioux City (converting those already in the market)
  - New customers in Sioux Falls (building awareness from a low base)
  - **Younger customers, particularly those 34 and under** — explicitly identified as an underserved and strategically important demographic
  - Commercial and agricultural borrowers (dedicated focus group segment)
  - Mortgage and wealth management clients

## Competitive Positioning & Differentiation

SNB's core differentiation rests on several pillars:

- **Relationship banking and personal service:** Described consistently as "second to none" by employees and deeply appreciated by existing customers. Long-tenured staff enables multi-generational relationships.
- **Stability and trust:** The 134-year history, conservative credit management, and strong capital base are perceived as meaningful differentiators — particularly valued by customers 35+.
- **Community investment:** SNB is recognized for its community orientation, nonprofit support, and local leadership — a strength that resonates with customers and some noncustomers.
- **Professional standards:** The bank's dress code (suits/formal business attire) is recognized as a visible market signal of professionalism and higher standards.
- **Wealth Management capability:** A distinct business line with its own team, suggesting meaningful product breadth beyond basic retail banking.

### Competitive Set
The report does not name specific bank competitors by name in the summarized sections, but references:
- **Large national/regional banks** (implied by noncustomer commentary about online capabilities and technology expectations)
- **A former "Security Bank"** in Sioux Falls (defunct) and a **new "Security Bank"** entering Sioux Falls simultaneously — creating direct name-confusion risk for SNB in the growth market
- **Peer community banks** (referenced in the context of capital and financial performance comparisons)

### Perceived Weaknesses Relative to Competitors
- Logo and visual identity perceived as **less credible and professional** than competitors by noncustomers — actively deterring consideration
- **Technology and digital banking perception gap** — noncustomers in Sioux City explicitly stated the logo gave the impression "they cannot do what I need online," indicating that visual identity is conflating with technology capability perceptions
- **Brand awareness deficit in Sioux Falls** leaves SNB vulnerable to competitors with stronger name recognition in that market

## Market Dynamics

### Economic Conditions
The report does not provide specific macroeconomic data for Sioux City or Sioux Falls markets. However, contextual indicators include:
- Sioux Falls is characterized as a **growth market**, implying favorable economic expansion and population/business growth relative to the legacy market
- The Siouxland region's economy has agricultural exposure (dedicated Commercial/Ag focus group), suggesting SNB has meaningful ag lending concentration

### Demographic Threats
- **Aging customer base** is the single most cited structural risk — if SNB cannot attract younger customers, organic deposit and loan growth will face long-term pressure
- **Generational banking behavior shift** — Millennials are more willing than prior generations to switch financial institutions and place higher value on digital capabilities and community involvement (cited from Deloitte Center for Banking Solutions research)

### Opportunities
- **Sioux Falls market growth:** The growth market provides a platform for meaningful new customer acquisition, commercial banking expansion, and long-term revenue diversification
- **Millennial/younger demographic acquisition:** SNB's community orientation and relationship banking model aligns with stated Millennial preferences — the brand just needs modernization to communicate this effectively
- **Digital banking investment:** Closing the perceived technology gap would remove a documented barrier to noncustomer conversion
- **Logo/identity evolution:** Updating the visual identity is framed as a direct mechanism to remove barriers to noncustomer consideration — analogous to facility and technology investment

## Brand Perception

### Customer Views (Sioux City & Sioux Falls)
Existing customers are **highly loyal and emotionally attached** to SNB — driven almost entirely by **people and service quality**, not the visual identity or brand aesthetics. Common customer themes:
- Deep trust in specific bankers and relationships spanning decades
- Appreciation for the bank's conservative, stable management
- Comfort with the bank's longevity and community presence
- **Logo perception is negative even among loyalists:** Described as "ancient," "archaic," "sedentary," "boring," and "1965" — though many loyal customers said "don't change it" out of attachment to familiarity
- Sioux Falls customers noted the logo looked "dated," "cold," "not welcoming," and "disproportional"

### Employee Views (Sioux City & Sioux Falls)
Employees are **highly loyal** and proud of SNB's people and service culture. However, even this group — the most likely to defend the status quo — offered strikingly candid criticism of the logo:
- "Dated," "tired," "lacks color and pop," "outdated," "looks like old Word art," "not memorable"
- Pride expressed in the lit SNB sign at night — indicating emotional connection to the brand despite aesthetic criticism
- Employees clearly feel the bank's service and culture are superior but that the logo is a liability

### Noncustomer Views (Sioux City & Sioux Falls)
Noncustomers were the **most critical group** and represent the primary conversion target. Their logo perceptions are a direct barrier to SNB consideration:
- Sioux City noncustomers: "So 1980s," "very stale," "industrial," "looks cheap," "gives the impression they are old and can't do what I need online," "it just looks cheap," "the SNB initials spell snob"
- Sioux Falls noncustomers: "Looks old," "I see a hardware store," "not professionally looking," "not financially prestigious looking," "the logo is so lame," unanimous thumbs down
- **Critical insight:** Noncustomers are drawing direct inferences about **technology capability and service quality** from the visual identity alone — the outdated logo is actively disqualifying SNB before any service interaction occurs

### Summary of Perception Gap
There is a significant disconnect between **SNB's actual capabilities** (strong capital, experienced staff, full-service including wealth management and mortgage, demonstrated performance) and **noncustomer perception** (old, limited, not technologically capable). The brand identity is the primary driver of this gap.

## Key Takeaways for Financial Analysis

### Deposit & Revenue Drivers
- **Core deposit base is concentrated in Sioux City** among a loyal but aging customer population — long-term deposit growth depends on successfully attracting younger demographics
- **Wealth Management** is an established business line, suggesting fee income diversification beyond net interest margin
- **Mortgage** is a distinct revenue segment with its own team
- **Commercial/Agricultural lending** is a core business line with dedicated relationship management — ag concentration warrants monitoring relative to regional farm economy conditions

### Branch & Market Plans
- **Sioux Falls is the priority growth market** — the bank has a 10-year presence but underdeveloped brand awareness; marketing investment is expected to increase materially
- The separate Sioux Falls charter structure may have implications for capital allocation, regulatory reporting, and intercompany dynamics
- No specific new branch opening plans are enumerated in the available report sections, but the growth market orientation implies continued physical or digital footprint expansion in Sioux Falls

### Financial Goals & Strategic Priorities (Inferred)
- Maintain **high-performance financial metrics** relative to peer banks — explicitly cited as a source of organizational pride and a competitive differentiator with commercial customers
- **Grow the Sioux Falls operation** to a scale commensurate with the legacy market over time
- **Lower the average customer age** — a stated organizational objective that will require product, marketing, and digital investment
- **Protect Sioux City market share** while the brand evolves — the report cautions that loyal customers are attached to people/service, not the logo, and will adapt to brand evolution without meaningful attrition risk
- **Capital position is described as strong** and supportive of continued growth — organic growth is the primary mechanism cited, though the strong capital base could support acquisitive activity

### Logo/Rebranding Investment Implications
- The recommended **logo evolution** (not full replacement) will require capital expenditure for: new signage across all locations, corporate stationery, specialty items, and a full communications rollout to shareholders, employees, and customers
- FMS will develop a complete **brand standards system** including vertical/horizontal logo formats, color palette, typography, email signatures, and brand messaging — to be deployed consistently across both markets
- An **internal branding program** will accompany the external launch to ensure employee alignment with the evolved brand promise
