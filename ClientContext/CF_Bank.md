# C&F Bank — Client Context Document
**Prepared by FMS | Brand Discovery Summary | September 2023**

---

## Background & History

C&F Bank is a community bank with approximately **95 years of operating history** as of 2023. The institution has demonstrated notable leadership stability, having had **only four presidents** in its entire history — a point of pride cited repeatedly by executives and retail bankers as evidence of institutional continuity and conservative stewardship.

The bank originated as a predominantly **consumer-oriented institution** but has evolved significantly into a **commercial banking operation**, developing particular strength in acquisition and development (A&D) lending and builder line lending. It has also built out a diversified financial services structure encompassing banking, mortgage, and wealth management — a differentiation point relative to many peer community banks.

C&F operates **31 branch locations** across Virginia markets.

---

## Geographic Footprint & Market Characteristics

C&F Bank maintains a multi-market Virginia footprint with established presence and active growth targets in the following geographies:

- **Charlottesville**
- **Metro Richmond** (including all surrounding counties)
- **Fredericksburg**
- **Williamsburg**
- **Peninsula**
- **Tidewater area / Southside / Norfolk / Virginia Beach** (targeted for mortgage and wealth expansion)

The bank's self-described market entry approach is **non-aggressive** — leadership acknowledges that in newer markets, brand awareness is low and the bank does not make a forceful push to establish presence. This is identified as both a cultural characteristic and a competitive liability in growth markets. The Richmond market in particular is noted as underpenetrated from an advertising standpoint, with growth to date driven primarily by **word of mouth**.

---

## Organizational Structure & Leadership

C&F operates as a **multi-entity corporate structure**, internally referred to as running as **"four individual companies"** — the bank, C&F Mortgage, C&F Wealth Management, and an implied fourth division. This structure has created meaningful internal friction:

- **Siloed cultures**: Mortgage in particular is cited as having a distinctly different internal culture from the rest of the organization.
- **Cross-referral resistance**: Branch personnel have historically been reluctant to refer customers to wealth management, with wealth management leadership noting that "some branch people don't think they are doing the bank any favors by getting them to us."
- **No centralized CRM**: The absence of a CRM system compounds cross-sell and cross-referral coordination challenges.

Business lines include:
- **Retail/Consumer Banking** (31 locations)
- **Business/Commercial Banking** (including Treasury Services)
- **C&F Mortgage** (B2C and referral-based mortgage origination)
- **C&F Wealth Management** (currently underpenetrated relative to bank footprint; no history of proactive communication to existing wealth customers)

Human Resources is an active stakeholder in the brand process, with employer brand development cited as an explicit goal — the bank is facing talent recruitment and retention challenges and views the brand refresh as an opportunity to address its external employment value proposition.

## Strategic Context

### Why the Rebrand
C&F engaged FMS in **September 2023** to develop a comprehensive brand and marketing strategy. The impetus for the engagement is multi-dimensional:

1. **Brand fragmentation**: The multi-entity structure has produced inconsistent brand presentation and a lack of unified identity in the market.
2. **Brand awareness deficit**: Despite 95 years of operation, awareness among non-customers — even in core markets — is strikingly low. One retail banker noted driving past C&F signage approximately **200 times** before joining the company without registering the brand.
3. **Outdated brand language**: The current mission, vision, and values were described by a 25-year employee as unrecitable and requiring "a translator." The existing tagline **"Focused On You"** is retained and considered non-negotiable; the broader brand language is not.
4. **Growth ambitions**: The bank has set aggressive deposit and loan growth targets requiring elevated market visibility and more systematic marketing infrastructure.
5. **Employer brand need**: An aging workforce and talent pipeline concerns require the bank to compete more actively for qualified employees.

### Growth Strategy
The 36-month strategic horizon (through approximately late 2026) is organized around:
- Deposit growth with geographic prioritization
- Loan volume expansion
- Cross-company referral and cross-sell improvement
- Treasury Services adoption among commercial clients
- Mortgage volume diversification beyond existing referral networks
- Wealth management expansion via branch integration

### Protected Brand Elements
- **C&F name and logo**: Sacred — not subject to change
- **Tagline "Focused On You."**: Retained as-is
- **Brand attributes** (from a 2015 brand study, confirmed still accurate): *Caring, Personal, Competitive, Responsible, Trustworthy, Professional*
- **Open for evolution**: Brand architecture, visual style, and color palette

## Competitive Positioning & Differentiation

C&F's core competitive position centers on **high-touch personal service** delivered by genuinely engaged employees, within the context of a **financially stable, long-tenured community institution** offering a **full suite of financial services** uncommon among true community banks.

Key differentiators cited:
- **Personal accessibility**: Customers can reach any employee — by call, text, or email — and receive substantive help. This direct accessibility is positioned as a major competitive advantage over larger institutions.
- **Consultative approach**: Bankers describe spending an hour with a customer working through their situation with no expectation of immediate return ("it may be a year from now, but we'll figure it out").
- **Physical community presence**: Employees show up to community events beyond check-writing sponsorships — soup kitchens, local events — described as a "blue-collar" authenticity to community engagement.
- **Diversified business model**: Unlike many community banks, C&F offers banking, mortgage, and wealth management, providing stability across economic cycles.
- **Small bank with big bank expertise**: Particularly in commercial lending, A&D, and first-time homebuyer products.

**Perceived positioning liability**: C&F is currently perceived externally as a **retail bank that does business banking** — a framing that undersells its commercial capabilities and limits business development opportunities.

## Market Dynamics

### Competitive Threats
- **Larger banks**: Cited as technology leaders with capabilities C&F lacks (Zelle for business accounts, online loan applications, robust digital account opening). Technology gap is acknowledged as a significant competitive vulnerability.
- **Digital/fintech friction**: Online account opening via Fiserv is described internally as "clunky" and "tired." Customer surveys confirm demand for improved digital services.
- **Speed disadvantage**: C&F's deliberate, conservative decision-making process is acknowledged to have cost the bank business — particularly in competitive commercial lending situations where speed matters.

### Opportunities
- **Underpenetrated existing customer base**: Cross-sell ratios appear low, and wealth management has done **no proactive communication** to existing bank customers who may be wealth-eligible.
- **Treasury Services**: Underpenetrated among existing commercial relationships; targeted for increased adoption.
- **B2C Mortgage**: C&F Mortgage has been heavily reliant on referral channels; a shift toward direct consumer marketing in Charlottesville, Fredericksburg, and Richmond represents a growth lever.
- **Brand clarity as growth enabler**: Internal consensus is that if C&F can get prospects "in the room," it wins the business — the challenge is awareness and consideration, not conversion.

## Brand Perception

### Customer / Market Perception
- In established markets: C&F is known and trusted; the personal service reputation is validated.
- In newer/growth markets (Richmond, Fredericksburg, Charlottesville): Brand awareness is low. The bank has not invested in advertising and relies on word of mouth, limiting growth velocity.
- Customers report **inconsistent branch experiences** — quality varies meaningfully by location, and some branches currently lack managers.
- Digital experience is a noted pain point in customer surveys.

### Employee Perception
- Long-tenured employees express pride in C&F's stability and community identity.
- There is internal resistance to change, particularly among veteran staff, creating friction for new initiatives.
- The multi-entity structure creates tribalism: business lines are protective of their customer relationships and skeptical of cross-referral value.
- Brand language (mission/vision/values) is not internalized — employees cannot recite or articulate it.
- Marketing campaigns are not effectively communicated to branch staff prior to launch.

### Non-Customer / Prospect Perception
- Awareness is demonstrably low even in core markets — the 200-drive-by anecdote is illustrative.
- C&F is perceived primarily as a retail/consumer bank, underselling its commercial and wealth capabilities.
- The employer brand is not well-developed, limiting talent attraction in competitive labor markets.

## Key Takeaways for Financial Analysis

### Deposit Strategy
- **Primary goal**: Average annual deposit growth exceeding **5%** over the 36-month horizon.
- **Branch-level target**: Average branch deposits exceeding **$100MM**, with a floor of **$40MM per branch** — management acknowledges this will be driven by both growing balances (numerator) and branch rationalization decisions (denominator).
- **DDA growth**: Average annual net new DDA growth target of **>3%**.
- Geographic deposit focus: Charlottesville, Metro Richmond and surrounding counties, Fredericksburg, Williamsburg, and Peninsula are the priority deposit growth markets.

### Loan Growth
- **Target**: Average annual loan growth exceeding **10%** — notably more aggressive than the deposit growth target, implying planned expansion of the loan-to-deposit ratio or reliance on wholesale funding to bridge the gap.
- Particular strength and growth focus in commercial/A&D lending and builder lines.

### Branch Plans
- No explicit branch openings or closures mentioned in the discovery report, but the "average branch deposits >$100MM / all over $40MM" metric suggests underperforming branches may face scrutiny. The consistency challenge (some branches lacking managers) and the 31-location footprint suggest rationalization conversations are likely.

### Cross-Sell / Revenue Diversification
- **Cross-company closed referrals** are an explicit 36-month goal — currently identified as broken due to cultural silos.
- **Treasury Services adoption** is a specific commercial banking revenue target.
- **Wealth Management integration** via securities-licensed branch personnel is a stated initiative — the current lack of any proactive wealth customer communication represents a significant unrealized revenue opportunity.

### Marketing Infrastructure Gap
- The marketing function is described as having **outgrown the marketing team** — a structural capacity issue, not just a strategy issue.
- The company lacks a **CRM**, limiting systematic relationship management, pipeline tracking, and cross-sell execution.
- No text-based customer communication capability exists despite customer demand.
- The bank's media investment in growth markets (particularly Richmond) is characterized as minimal — growth has been organic/referral-based, which will be insufficient to hit stated targets.

### Financial Profile Context
- The bank's conservative credit culture and balance sheet strength are consistently cited as foundational assets — the "thoughtful, intentional and conservative" posture suggests strong asset quality metrics.
- The diversified business model (banking + mortgage + wealth) is highlighted as a cycle-management tool.
- The 10% annual loan growth target against a conservative credit culture will be a key tension to monitor, particularly in a higher-rate environment.
